THIRD-PARTY SOFTWARE NOTICES AND INFORMATION

Fresco.Brix is a CodeBrix.Platform application inspired by, and in large part
ported from, Frescobaldi -- the LilyPond music editor by Wilbert Berendsen and
contributors. It also carries ports of python-ly and qpageview, data files
taken from Frescobaldi, and a set of hyphenation dictionaries that are the work
of many other people.

This document is the attribution and licensing-compliance record for the
application. Every piece of third-party code, data or asset that has been
adapted, copied, generated from, or bundled with Fresco.Brix is recorded here
with its authors, its upstream location, its license, and the files in this
repository derived from it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THIS FILE IS MAINTAINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. This is a LIVING LEDGER, kept in step with the code. A wave that brings in
     third-party material adds its entry in the same session.

  2. Fresco.Brix's OWN sources are GPL-3.0-or-later; every file carries that
     header. The application as CONVEYED is GPL-3.0-only, because
     CodeBrix.LilyPort is (ruling FR1, and LilyPort's own notices file records
     why). The two statements look contradictory and are not: the aggregate
     takes the narrower of the terms it combines.

  3. Ported files carry a `//was previously:' note on their namespace line
     naming the upstream file they came from, so provenance is checkable from
     the source as well as from here.

  4. Material is filed by its ACTUAL license, not by the project it arrived
     with. Section 5 is the case in point: the hyphenation dictionaries came
     from Frescobaldi, and not one of them is under Frescobaldi's license.

  5. Section 5 also records what is NOT settled. A row whose license cannot be
     established from what upstream ships says so, rather than being assumed
     into the same bucket as its neighbours.

  6. Sections are added as waves land. Rows still owed at the time of writing
     are listed in section 9, so the gaps are visible rather than implied.

  The operative grant:

     projects, and to modify the SoundFont bank or its packaging to suit your

  Permissive, so unlike the hyphenation dictionaries of section 5 this bank
  needs no aggregate argument: it may be part of the combined GPL-3.0-only
  work outright. It is nonetheless SHIPPED as a separable file that the
  application merely reads, because Fresco.Brix lets the user choose any .sf2
  or .sfz they like -- the folder can be emptied and the application still
  runs.

  ONE OBLIGATION, and it is met by vendoring: "If you plan to feature
  GeneralUser GS on your own website, please do not link directly to my
  download files. Either link to my website, or provide your own local copy
  instead." This repository provides its own local copy.

  DISCLOSED BY THE AUTHOR, recorded here rather than paraphrased away:

    "Many of the samples are original, but some were taken from other banks
     freely (and legally) available on the Internet from various SoundFont
     websites. Because GeneralUser GS originated as a personal project with no
     intention for publication, I cannot be 100% sure where all of the samples
     originated, although I do know that none of them came from commercially
     published SoundFont packages or sample CDs. ... If you become aware of
     any restricted samples being used in GeneralUser GS, please let me know
     so I can replace them."

  He adds that no complaint has reached him since the original publication in
  2000, nor, as far as he is aware, any of the commercial products that use
  it. The uncertainty is the author's own statement and is reproduced so a
  reader can weigh it rather than discover it.

  ⚠ TWO PARTS OF THE UPSTREAM REPOSITORY ARE DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED, and this
  is why only the five files above are vendored rather than the distribution
  as a whole:

  1. THE "demo MIDIs" FOLDER (nine .mid files and nine .ogg renderings,
     31 MB). The GeneralUser GS license covers the SOUNDFONT BANK and its
     samples; it says nothing about those musical compositions, and several
     are plainly third-party copyrighted works -- "Santa Claus is Comin' to
     Town" (Coots/Gillespie 1934, still in US copyright), "Umi no Mieru Machi"
     (Joe Hisaishi 1989), and "Bond" (the Monty Norman theme). Nothing in
     Fresco.Brix reads them. Vendoring them would put unlicensed third-party
     music into this repository, which is precisely what choosing this bank
     was meant to avoid.
  2. THE "support" FOLDER: launcher configuration for FluidSynth, a different
     application Fresco.Brix does not use, including a macOS .app bundle
     containing a compiled AppleScript droplet binary that is not Collins' to
     license, plus a Cakewalk .ins file and a timing-test recording.

  The standing rule for this folder: keep the licensed work and the documents
  that describe it, and keep nothing whose license cannot be pointed at. If
  any excluded file is ever wanted, it needs its own per-file audit first --
  the way section 5 treats the hyphenation dictionaries.

  ⚠ THE FILE'S OWN INAM STRING IS STALE and reads "GeneralUser GS 2.0.3 BETA",
  with an ICRD date of 2024-10-15. Upstream last refreshed those strings at
  v2.0.1; its changelog dates v2.0.3 to 2026-02-22 as a normal release. No UI
  may surface INAM as the bank's version, or the application will report a
  beta to its users.

  WHY A BANK SHIPS AT ALL: Frescobaldi ships none, because it does not
  synthesize -- it sends MIDI to an external port and tells the user to run
  TiMidity or FluidSynth. Ruling FR6 removed that mechanism in favour of
  in-process synthesis with no MIDI ports, so upstream's empty state is not
  available here as parity behaviour. Shipping a bank is a deliberate,
  necessary divergence from Frescobaldi.

  CONSIDERED AND REJECTED: TimGM6mb (5.97 MB) is what Doom.Brix uses and was
  the obvious first candidate, but Debian's DEP-5 record for it reads
  "License: GPL-2" -- not "GPL-2+" -- and the debian/* rows in that same file
  read "GPL-3+", so the packagers withheld the "+" deliberately. In a
  GPL-3.0-only application that would have been a fourth GPL-2-only row
  carried on section 5's aggregate reading, for the file a user hears first.
  MuseScore_General (MIT) and FluidR3_GM are 206 MB and 145 MB as uncompressed
  SF2; every permissive bank small enough to bundle (FluidR3Mono, the
  MuseScore General variants) is SF3, whose Vorbis-compressed sample data
  CodeBrix.Audio does not read.
