THIRD-PARTY SOFTWARE NOTICES AND INFORMATION

Fresco.Brix is a CodeBrix.Platform application inspired by, and in large part
ported from, Frescobaldi -- the LilyPond music editor by Wilbert Berendsen and
contributors. It also carries ports of python-ly and qpageview, data files
taken from Frescobaldi, and a set of hyphenation dictionaries that are the work
of many other people.

This document is the attribution and licensing-compliance record for the
application. Every piece of third-party code, data or asset that has been
adapted, copied, generated from, or bundled with Fresco.Brix is recorded here
with its authors, its upstream location, its license, and the files in this
repository derived from it.

HOW THIS FILE IS MAINTAINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. This is a LIVING LEDGER, kept in step with the code. A wave that brings in
     third-party material adds its entry in the same session.

  2. Fresco.Brix's OWN sources are GPL-3.0-or-later; every file carries that
     header. The application as CONVEYED is GPL-3.0-only, because
     CodeBrix.LilyPort is (ruling FR1, and LilyPort's own notices file records
     why). The two statements look contradictory and are not: the aggregate
     takes the narrower of the terms it combines.

  3. Ported files carry a `//was previously:' note on their namespace line
     naming the upstream file they came from, so provenance is checkable from
     the source as well as from here.

  4. Material is filed by its ACTUAL license, not by the project it arrived
     with. Section 5 is the case in point: the hyphenation dictionaries came
     from Frescobaldi, and not one of them is under Frescobaldi's license.

  5. Section 5 also records what is NOT settled. A row whose license cannot be
     established from what upstream ships says so, rather than being assumed
     into the same bucket as its neighbours.

  6. Sections are added as waves land. Rows still owed at the time of writing
     are listed in the last section, so the gaps are visible rather than
     implied.

  HOW COMPLETE THEY ARE, AND WHY SOME OF THE INTERFACE STAYS ENGLISH

  A translation a PO file marks "fuzzy" is one its translator has not confirmed
  since the English changed. GNU msgfmt leaves fuzzy entries out of a compiled

  little: Galician and Turkish come out largely English, and German and Italian
  are complete. That is upstream's own state and nothing was done to it.

  Separately, 196 msgids in this application are Fresco.Brix's OWN and match no
  catalog by design -- the strings ruling FR13 rewrote so that no menu, button
  or tooltip names LilyPond, the strings ruling FR9 rewrote because this
  application is not Frescobaldi, and the strings that describe controls or
  features upstream does not have. They show in English in every language until
  somebody translates them, and they are listed one per line, with their
  reason, in tools/i18nharvest/renamed-strings.tsv.

  Regenerate the catalogs with tools/i18nharvest/harvest.py; it reads the
  read-only reference checkout, ships nothing, and is not part of any build.

WHY A GPL-3-ONLY APPLICATION MAY CARRY THEM

  The GNU FDL is NOT a software license and is NOT GPL-compatible in either
  direction. This is the same arrangement section 5's hyphenation dictionaries
  ship under, and it is what GPLv3 section 5 permits for separate works
  aggregated on one distribution medium:

  - The manuals are DOCUMENTS, not source and not part of the program. Nothing
    links against them, nothing #includes them, and no build step reads them.

  - They live in ONE clearly separated folder that contains no source, and
    COPYING.FDL sits in that folder, which is what the licence itself requires
    of anyone who distributes the work.

  - The folder can be emptied and the application still runs: Help > LilyPort
    Documentation then reports that no manuals are installed. A packager who
    must not ship FDL material can drop the folder and ship the rest.

  - They are conveyed UNMODIFIED, under their own titles and with their own
    copyright and licence statements intact -- which is also why the
    documentation panel lists each one by its own title, several of which say
    "LilyPond" (ruling FD12, Jeremy, 2026-08-21).

  ⚠ THE STANDING PROHIBITION REACHES HERE UNCHANGED. No text from these files
  may be copied into source files or XML doc comments anywhere in this
  repository. That is CodeBrix.LilyPort's own rule (its THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt
  section 4) and it applies to Fresco.Brix for the same reason.

  ⚠ A NOTED DISCREPANCY, recorded rather than resolved, and inherited from

11.3 One file diverges -- tools-score-wizard.svg (ruling FR14)

  ⚠ DELIBERATE DIVERGENCE FROM UPSTREAM. Thirty-six of the thirty-eight files
  are byte-for-byte copies. The two copies of tools-score-wizard.svg are not.
  //was previously: twenty-eight of thirty, before board wave W15 added four
  names to each set.

  Upstream's file is 6,474,769 bytes in the Light set and 6,474,783 in the
  Dark set -- 228,519 lines each, for a 24-pixel icon whose whole drawing is
  7 <path>, 2 <rect> and 28 <g>. The remaining ~228,000 lines are 20,760
  orphan <inkscape:path-effect> elements an Inkscape session accumulated
  inside <defs> and never dropped.

  tools/iconclean/iconclean.py removes them, in two steps that cannot change
  the drawing: Inkscape's own plain-SVG writer with its unused-definition
  sweep (`inkscape --export-plain-svg --vacuum-defs'), which drops every
  element and attribute in Inkscape's private XML namespaces -- namespaces an
  SVG renderer is required to ignore -- and then a pass that deletes groups
  with no children. The shipped files are 2,761 and 2,742 bytes.

  The claim is PROVEN, not asserted: upstream's original is recorded
  gzip-compressed at tests/Fresco.Brix.Core.Tests/fixtures/icons/, and
  IconThemeTests renders it and the shipped file through this application's
  own renderer at 24, 48 and 96 pixels and asserts that every pixel agrees.
  It does, exactly -- the largest channel difference is zero, in both sets, at
  all three sizes.

  The divergence is written up as a bug report against Frescobaldi in
  STATUS_frescobrix_w14_2026-09-02.txt.
